Legal facts come from the Civil Associations Act, Foundations Act, Company Act, Income Tax Act and Public Interest Fundraising Act. The setup times are estimates, not law. A religious group is not suitable: it is only for genuinely religious activity and is excluded from the fundraising law.

Choosing the form

Question 1

Is tax exemption needed?

If no: a company 有限公司, for paid work only.

Question 2

Can an endowment be funded?

If yes: a foundation 財團法人.

Question 3

Are 30 adult founders ready?

If yes: a local association 社團法人. If no: build the community first, or use a fiscal sponsor 靠行.

Questions 2 and 3 apply when tax exemption is needed.

4

Walk through the decision chart. The founder has no large endowment, so a foundation is out. Thirty founders is the real gate for an association; until then, a fiscal sponsor (靠行) means an existing public-benefit body hosts the project.

Possible Structure

A local association carries the mission. An optional company beside it carries paid work.

Local association 社團法人

  • Community, grants and donations
  • 30 or more adult founders
  • Tax-exempt potential
  • Public fundraising with permission

Company 有限公司, optional

  • Paid training and consulting
  • 1 or more shareholders, 1 to 3 directors
  • Capital paid in full
  • Not tax-exempt, because it is profit-making

An association may also charge fees within its charter purpose. To be confirmed.

5

Recommended path from the research notes: build a community, gather 30 founders, then register a county or city level association. A small limited company can run beside it if paid work grows. How an association taxes a revenue-generating arm is still an open question.

Founders must be 18 or older under Civil Code Art. 12. The charter must state name, purpose, organizational area, seat, tasks and structure. The point about a European mission and a local charter area is my observation, not from the source notes; confirm with the Social Affairs Bureau.

8

A legal person is not formed until it is registered (Civil Code Art. 30), and a public-interest association needs the authority's permission before registration (Art. 46). Only public-interest associations, foundations, public schools and administrative corporations can fundraise publicly. Religious fundraising is outside the Act.

9

Kaohsiung is stricter than a plain 30-founder count: city-level groups need founders spread across at least one third of the city's districts, with a distribution table. The Bureau's 人民團體科 can be reached at 337-3409, 337-3673 or 3344885 ext. 1. For a district-level group, founders must live or work in that district, spread across a third of its villages (里). Founders registered elsewhere but working in Kaohsiung attach proof of workplace. Only this one page has been read; the Ministry of the Interior rules it links are not yet read.
